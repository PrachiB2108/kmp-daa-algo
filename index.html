2. Benchmark Results

Measured with the Python reference implementation across text lengths and alphabet sizes (DNA = 4 symbols, English prose ≈ 27 symbols, synthetic uniform alphabets up to 94 symbols). Full methodology and raw CSVs are in the benchmark-data/ folder.

Key findings

  • At 100,000 characters of DNA text, KMP performs ≈1.24 comparisons per character (bounded by its ≤2n guarantee) vs. Boyer-Moore's ≈0.33 — about 3.7× fewer comparisons.
  • On English-like prose (larger alphabet), Boyer-Moore's advantage widens further (≈0.12 comparisons/char) because bad-character skips get larger as symbols repeat less often.
  • KMP's comparison count stays almost flat across alphabet sizes — its guarantee depends on pattern self-similarity, not alphabet size — while Boyer-Moore improves sharply as the alphabet grows, then plateaus past ≈30 symbols.
  • On an adversarial "aaa...ab" pattern, KMP's comparisons barely change (true O(n) worst case), confirming it never degrades.
